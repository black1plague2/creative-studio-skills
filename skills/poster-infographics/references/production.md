# Posters and infographics: production rules

These rules apply to the selected task and supplied brief. Use judgment for reversible choices; preserve explicit user requirements.

## 1. Separate information layout from visual style

Separate information layout from visual style. A comparison, sequence, map, hierarchy, or quantitative plot needs a structure that preserves its meaning; paper texture, typography and illustration express the identity.

## 2. Choose a dominant focal point without hiding the evidence

Choose a dominant focal point without hiding the evidence. A giant headline that relegates necessary data to unreadable sidebars fails a research poster. Design for scanning and for a closer second reading.

## 3. For a technical plate, place real actors/objects at the point of explanation

For a technical plate, place real actors/objects at the point of explanation. Arrows must have an unambiguous direction and labels. Show branches, inputs, outputs and constraints rather than decorative connectors.

## 4. Control density through grouping, type hierarchy and visual panels

Control density through grouping, type hierarchy and visual panels. Preserve meaningful caveats and units. Do not make an infographic by copying a document into tiny boxes.

## 5. Use original art or supplied/cleared assets

Use original art or supplied/cleared assets. If a generation route is requested, use an actual image tool. For exact charts, brands, logos or diagram labels, choose a controlled composition unless the user explicitly wants an all-generated image.

## 6. Generate meaningful scene illustrations: a technician installing hardware, a borrower receiving an object, or a before/after process

Generate meaningful scene illustrations: a technician installing hardware, a borrower receiving an object, or a before/after process. Avoid stock symbols that do not explain the subject.

## 7. For print, establish final physical size, margins/bleed, effective raster resolution at placement, color/export needs, fonts, and printer proof requirements

For print, establish final physical size, margins/bleed, effective raster resolution at placement, color/export needs, fonts, and printer proof requirements. Do not infer print suitability from an on-screen screenshot.

## 8. Test a headline/content region at the intended reading distance or output scale

Test a headline/content region at the intended reading distance or output scale. Check labels, line crossings, icon semantics and essential evidence. Aesthetic novelty cannot excuse unreadability.

## 9. Package digital and print variants deliberately

Package digital and print variants deliberately. Preserve the original aspect and content. Include an accessible text summary where the image is the sole carrier of important information.

## 10. Deliver production source when feasible, final exports, and a short record of any print/profile or source limitations

Deliver production source when feasible, final exports, and a short record of any print/profile or source limitations. Do not copy upstream templates or assets merely because their repo is public.
