# Creative portfolio and media: show the work and credit the work

Use for portfolios, films, podcasts, visual case studies, editorial projects and creative campaigns. Distinguish author contribution, collaboration, original work, licensed material and speculative work. A striking presentation cannot substitute for rights or credits.

## Editable brief

- Work, audience and intended commission/viewing decision: [fill].
- Creator's exact role and collaborator credits: [fill].
- Released, commissioned, speculative or fictional status: [fill].
- Supplied image/audio/footage rights and permitted distribution: [fill].
- Narrative tone, content boundaries and medium: [fill].
- Required samples, contact and final delivery: [fill].

Ask: What did this creator actually contribute? Which assets are cleared for this use? What should the audience view or do next, and is the work released or speculative? Do not invent awards, clients, festivals, endorsements or audience statistics.

## Creative constraints

Let real work samples drive hierarchy and preserve their identity. Use one dominant visual reference with supporting roles rather than averaging inspirations. A concept storyboard remains concept art, not evidence of filmed material. Do not clone an identifiable person's voice from the portfolio brief. Narration should feel like a creator explaining a choice; audition and listen rather than selecting a “human” preset by label.

## Task adaptations

| Group | Domain-specific treatment |
|---|---|
| Brief and language | Separate role, rationale and status; do not imply production/selection achievements. |
| Slide and poster | Complete GPT 16:9 illustrated pitch posters use cinematic light continuity with readable exact titles and status; a single film poster has its own intended dimensions. |
| Reading documents | Treatment organizes logline, visual approach, keyframes, production questions and credits; a PDF is not actual video production. |
| Website and app | Portfolio prioritizes work viewing and accessible media controls; if an editor tool is requested, route its task/states to app-interfaces. |
| Video, voice and interaction | Temporal changes show the story rather than slide transitions. Listen to creator-style narration and check picture/audio together. Clickable frame comparison has a meaningful light-change control. |
| Identity, assets and campaign | Use cohesive keyframes and accurate credit blocks; channel variants preserve concept status and work rights. |
| Data and evidence | No audience/engagement claim from fabricated analytics; supplied real metrics need platform/date/context. |

Review failure example: portfolio labels a speculative storyboard “Award-winning film.” Repair copy, credits and dependent promotional assets; a small disclaimer does not cure the headline.
