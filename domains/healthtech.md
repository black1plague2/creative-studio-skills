# Healthtech: administrative clarity before medical authority

Use for appointment, care-navigation, patient-education or health-product creatives. First distinguish administrative workflow, general educational content and clinical claims. This pack gives creative-production constraints; it is not a source of medical guidance. Any real clinical recommendation or efficacy statement needs appropriate current evidence and qualified review for its intended use.

Material unanswered questions: Which exact administrative task or medical topic should the artifact explain? What authoritative supplied source supports any clinical statement? Is this a concept demonstration or a real service, and which patient details may appear? Do not reopen answers already provided.

## Creative constraints

Use calm but specific scenes and clear actions; a blue gradient and stethoscope do not establish trust. Represent people with dignity, varied adult contexts and readable language. Do not imply that every person is a clinician because of clothing. Separate requested appointment, confirmed appointment and cancellation. Keep important qualifications beside the relevant claim. Do not generate realistic patient records, clinician endorsements, certification badges, outcome metrics or therapeutic promises from thin source material.

## Filled fictional exercise — VisitBridge

All facts in this exercise are invented scenario inputs, not a real service. VisitBridge is a scheduling prototype for adult clinic visitors. Proposed flow: choose a department → request an available slot → see a request receipt. Staff confirmation is outside the prototype; a request receipt is not confirmation. No symptom assessment, diagnosis, treatment, patient record or clinical improvement claim is included. Audience: first-time adult visitors. Purpose: explain what happens after requesting a slot. Exact draft copy: **“Request a visit. Know what happens next.”** Status label: **“Fictional scheduling prototype.”** No clinical-data source exists, so no clinical claim is made.

## Task adaptations

| Group | Apply to this domain and exercise |
|---|---|
| Brief and language | Ledger distinguishes request versus confirmation; use plain action language and a truthful “Request a slot” CTA. |
| Slide and poster | Illustrate visitor, calendar and receipt as a coherent journey. GPT deck images are complete 16:9 posters; avoid anatomically suggestive medical imagery for this administrative task. |
| Reading documents | One-page visitor guide explains steps, receipt meaning and unresolved staff follow-up; semantic headings, readable body type and qualifications near actions. |
| Website and app | Public page explains scheduling scope; product states include no slots, stale availability, invalid input and received request. Do not show a working clinical backend in a prototype. |
| Video, voice and interaction | Narration: “You’ve sent a request. The receipt tells you what happens next.” Listen for reassuring but non-authoritative delivery. Branching demo lets user choose a slot, then explains request status. |
| Identity, assets and campaign | Friendly editorial calendar/visitor motifs; invented illustration labeled as such. Campaign variants retain request/status qualification, not “instant appointment guaranteed.” |
| Data and evidence | No clinical, waiting-time or adoption chart without supplied data. If dates are illustrative, mark them as sample dates and avoid accidental real booking commitments. |

Review failure example: a success screen says “Appointment confirmed” although the source only promises a received request. This is a source/behavior blocker, repaired in copy and every dependent visual.
