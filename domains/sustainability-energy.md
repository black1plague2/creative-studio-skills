# Sustainability and energy: show the measurement boundary

Use for environmental initiatives, energy products, monitoring, technical pitches and impact communication. Separate measurement, estimate, scenario and proposed benefit. A green palette is not evidence of environmental improvement.

## Editable brief

- System/site, audience and intended decision: [fill].
- Source dataset, period, units and system boundary: [fill].
- Measured versus estimated variables: [fill].
- Baseline, assumptions, exclusions and uncertainty: [fill].
- Proposed versus implemented hardware/software: [fill].
- Artifact and viewing/print environment: [fill].

Ask: What is actually measured, over what interval and in which units? Against what valid baseline is the benefit compared? Which impact assumptions or excluded processes must be visible? No invented emission factors, savings, efficiency figures or certifications.

## Creative constraints

Illustrate the actual mechanism and topology: panel, meter, data path and viewer if the source supplies them. Label kilowatts versus kilowatt-hours correctly. Keep a technical plate dense where grouping supports comprehension; do not sacrifice the system's meaning for a sparse decorative poster. Illustrative terrain and sun motifs do not establish a location or real deployment.

## Filled fictional exercise — SunHarbor

Original rooftop-monitoring concept for a facilities student team. Proposed flow: a simulated meter provides daily energy readings → a local demo dashboard compares days. Synthetic exercise readings: Monday 12 kWh, Tuesday 15 kWh, Wednesday 9 kWh. Sum 36 kWh over three sample days; no claim of typical output, savings, emissions avoided or functioning hardware. Exact title: **“Read the output. Understand the pattern.”** Caption: **“Synthetic three-day example.”** No baseline or environmental-impact dataset is supplied.

## Task adaptations

| Group | Domain-specific treatment |
|---|---|
| Brief and language | Ledger separates proposed meter flow and synthetic energy readings; never replace kWh with kW. |
| Slide and poster | Complete GPT 16:9 posters illustrate roof-to-meter-to-dashboard relationship. Any exact graph is separately verified or uses an explicitly authorized deterministic exception. |
| Reading documents | Report page pairs reproducible table/plot with boundary/period/caveat; the fictional concept does not need invented impact pages. |
| Website and app | Public page claims visibility of a sample pattern, not savings; app handles missing day/readings and unit labels. |
| Video, voice and interaction | Show meter reading becoming a chart value at the spoken anchor; listen for “kilowatt-hours.” A viewer-controlled day selection changes a real labeled value. |
| Identity, assets and campaign | Rooftop hardware and measured-flow motifs communicate topic specificity; no borrowed certification seals or generic planet-saving claim. |
| Data and evidence | Three equally wide bars on a zero-baseline energy axis, source table preserved, total checked, no extrapolation to annual generation. |

Review failure example: campaign transforms 36 kWh sample total into “36 kg of carbon saved.” No conversion evidence exists; repair the claim rather than decorating it with a footnote.
