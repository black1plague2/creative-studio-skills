# Fintech: exact money and honest assumptions

Use for budgeting, financial-literacy, payment/product explanations and finance-related design. This is creative guidance, not financial advice or a regulatory checklist. Real terms, advice, legal obligations and product claims need current authoritative support for the intended jurisdiction/use.

## Editable brief

- Audience and money-related decision: [fill].
- Jurisdiction/currency/locale when material: [fill].
- Product and account status: [concept, sandbox or real service].
- Exact source values, date, units and assumptions: [fill].
- Charges, conditions, exclusions and uncertainty: [fill from source].
- Requested artifact, data handling and output: [fill].

Ask: Is this education, a product demonstration or personalized advice? Which currency, terms and jurisdiction actually apply? What source supports rates, savings, eligibility or return claims? Keep missing real terms unresolved rather than writing a confident placeholder.

## Creative constraints

Separate actual balances from synthetic examples and future scenarios. Distinguish amount, rate, annualization and time period. Show conditions beside claims, not hidden below a persuasive graphic. Avoid invented bank partnerships, guaranteed outcomes, regulatory badges, credit approvals and recommendations. A chart rising upward cannot imply an investment result when no investment data exists.

## Filled fictional exercise — PocketPlan

Original budgeting sandbox for adults learning category planning. Synthetic monthly example, in unspecified **sample currency units**: income 2,000; planned essentials 1,100; flexible spending 500; unallocated 400. Arithmetic: 2,000 − 1,100 − 500 = 400. This is not a real person's account, a suggested budget or evidence of savings. Proposed flow: enter a sample income → allocate categories → inspect the unallocated amount. No bank connection, payment, credit or investment feature. Exact draft copy: **“See where your sample budget goes.”** Figure caption: **“Synthetic monthly example; not a recommendation.”**

## Task adaptations

| Group | Domain-specific treatment |
|---|---|
| Brief and language | Ledger records amounts, units, period and synthetic status; “unallocated” does not become “profit” or “savings guaranteed.” |
| Slide and poster | GPT 16:9 illustrated posters explain categories with exact labels. Preserve deterministic supporting chart/data if quantitative fidelity matters; do not accept an attractive inaccurate model-drawn plot. |
| Reading documents | Explain assumptions before the example and include the checked equation; offer a clear table alongside a visual. |
| Website and app | Prototype labels sample inputs; states include invalid numeric entry and allocations above income; no real-account illusion or implied transaction. |
| Video, voice and interaction | Narration speaks “two thousand sample units,” pauses at the allocation consequence and is actually listened to. Interactive sliders recalculate correctly; default values remain labeled hypothetical. |
| Identity, assets and campaign | Concrete category objects rather than wealth/luxury imagery; variants carry the synthetic qualifier and avoid urgency about financial decisions. |
| Data and evidence | Validate sums, units and residual; preserve source precision; no conversion to real currency or percentage claim without explicit calculation/context. |

Review failure example: sample 400 residual is advertised as “Save 400 every month.” That changes a scenario into advice/guarantee and must be removed everywhere.
