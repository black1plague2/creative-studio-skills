# Personal and professional: specific contribution, verifiable claims

Use for resumes, professional bios, portfolios, proposals, personal learning artifacts and project summaries. Real credentials, dates, employers, results and authorship must come from the person/source. Do not invent achievements to make the narrative stronger.

## Editable brief

- Intended reader and decision: [recruit, commission, fund, introduce; fill].
- Verified role, project dates, contribution and result: [fill].
- Supplied resume/notes/work sample and permission scope: [fill].
- Contact details to publish or keep private: [fill].
- Tone, length, language and format requirements: [fill].
- Missing factual points requiring the person's input: [fill].

Ask: What did you personally do, and what evidence can be shown? Which dates/results/credentials are verified? Which contact details may appear in the intended output? A professional-looking document does not authorize public release of personal contact information.

## Creative constraints

Use concrete contributions and readable chronology. Distinguish team result from personal ownership and proposal from shipped work. Preserve job titles/dates exactly; clarify ambiguity rather than upgrading a title. No invented degrees, employment, clients, testimonials or measurable impact. Tailor hierarchy to the actual audience, not ornamental expertise badges.

## Task adaptations

| Group | Domain-specific treatment |
|---|---|
| Brief and language | Separate role, decisions and limits; strongest truthful contribution replaces vague “visionary designer” claims. |
| Slide and poster | Complete GPT 16:9 posters can tell a three-page case-study story using prototype scenes and exact contribution labels; do not imply those scenes are functioning product screenshots. |
| Reading documents | DOCX uses semantic headings for summary, contributions and source evidence; PDF case study keeps readable process/limitations and does not rasterize body text unnecessarily. |
| Website and app | Portfolio links work and protects contact preferences; personal tracker/product UI uses real states only where requested. |
| Video, voice and interaction | First-person script owns specific decisions, not imagined achievements. Listen for natural personal delivery. Clickable prototype is labeled and tested as a prototype. |
| Identity, assets and campaign | Visual identity expresses actual work; personal illustrations remain original and do not invent a photographic identity. Posts retain role/status and authorized contact scope. |
| Data and evidence | No impact chart without evidence; before/after comparison may show design differences without asserting measured usability gains. |

Review failure example: “Reduced booking errors by 40%” is added to an untested prototype case study. Remove it, retain the real design contribution and leave measured outcome unavailable.
