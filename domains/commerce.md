# Commerce: let the actual product carry the promise

Use for retail/product launches, storefronts, catalogs, packaging stories and campaign creatives. Product appearance, material, dimensions, availability and offer terms must come from source content. Generated scenes can communicate a concept but cannot silently become real product evidence.

## Editable brief

- Product/category, audience and purchase decision: [fill].
- Supplied photography versus illustrative asset route: [fill].
- Exact variant/material/dimension/care information: [fill].
- Current price, stock, shipping, returns and offer terms: [fill with dates].
- Status and intended CTA: [concept preview, enquiry or real purchase].
- Required formats/channels and text safe areas: [fill].

Ask: Which product details and images are real? Are customers purchasing now or viewing a concept? What exact price/availability/offer terms can be stated? Never infer stock, safety, material certification or delivery guarantees from the visual brief.

## Creative constraints

Honor real proportions, colors and variant names. Keep price and terms readable. No fabricated reviews, ratings, sold-out demand, scarcity, ethical claims or creator endorsements. Show truthful error/unavailable states and explain next action. A visually rich catalog still needs comparable specification fields and readable page rhythm.

## Filled fictional exercise — ClayNest

Original ceramics-shop concept, not an active business. Proposed catalog: a speckled cream mug, cobalt bud vase and terracotta bowl. These are illustration subjects, not verified physical products; no price, capacity, food-safety suitability, stock, shipping or returns data exists. Audience: homeware shoppers comparing style. Purpose: invite viewing the collection concept. Exact headline: **“Everyday forms. Quiet character.”** CTA: **“View the concept collection.”** Status: **“Fictional product illustrations.”**

## Task adaptations

| Group | Domain-specific treatment |
|---|---|
| Brief and language | Catalog copy describes visual form only; missing material/safety/capacity facts remain open. |
| Slide and poster | GPT 16:9 slides act as fully composed editorial product posters with complete exact lettering and varied arrangements; do not add made-up prices or sales badges. |
| Reading documents | A lookbook gives each object a consistent name and clear illustration status; a real catalog requires source-verified specs and terms. |
| Website and app | Preview page has working collection navigation; cart/checkout are excluded unless actually requested and supplied. Product unavailable/loading states are explicit in a real shop. |
| Video, voice and interaction | Gentle object rotation serves comparison, not proof of material quality. Listen for natural understated language. Interactive color choice only changes a labeled concept variant. |
| Identity, assets and campaign | Use surface texture, silhouette and photographic/illustration distinction consistently; channel variants keep the truthful concept CTA and crop whole handles/rims. |
| Data and evidence | No price comparison, sales ranking or environmental benefit chart without source evidence; illustrative assortment is not consumer demand data. |

Review failure example: a generated mug is labeled dishwasher-safe because it looks ceramic. Remove the unsupported product claim and ask for a real specification only when required for completion.
