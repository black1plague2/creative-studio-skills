# Industrial and scientific: preserve the mechanism and the evidence

Use for technical systems, laboratory workflows, engineering pitches, research communication and industrial interfaces. Distinguish measured result, model assumption, proposed mechanism and illustrative scene. This pack does not supply safety instructions, scientific validation or operating certification.

## Editable brief

- System/research question, audience expertise and decision: [fill].
- Source figure/data/protocol and exact version: [fill].
- Units, symbols, topology and component labels: [fill].
- Measurement/model/illustration status and uncertainty: [fill].
- Safety/operating content requiring authoritative source: [fill if relevant].
- Artifact, print/projected dimensions and detail density: [fill].

Ask: What exact relationship or finding must survive simplification? Which original data/figures and uncertainty are available? Is this explanatory material, a product concept or actual operating guidance? Missing safety limits and measured values remain unresolved.

## Creative constraints

Meaningful architecture diagrams and technical plates are welcome when faithful to the mechanism. Distinguish a label from a quantitative encoding. Preserve symbols, units, arrow direction and component topology. Generated plots are not quantitative evidence by default; use original figures/deterministic charts where needed and document any GPT-poster exception. Keep methods/limitations readable rather than using a decorative main message to shrink them away.

## Filled fictional exercise — BenchTrace

Original inventory-tracking concept for a university teaching lab. Proposed flow: scan a sample identifier → see its assigned shelf → record a moved location. Exercise identifiers BT-A and BT-B are synthetic; shelves A1 and B2 are fictional. No chemical/material content, handling instruction, calibration, analytical result, sensor or deployed integration exists. Exact copy: **“Know where the sample belongs.”** Status: **“Fictional inventory concept.”** Invalid/unknown identifier receives clear feedback without guessing a location.

## Task adaptations

| Group | Domain-specific treatment |
|---|---|
| Brief and language | Preserve sample-versus-result distinction and precise identifier strings; no reliability or safety benefit claim. |
| Slide and poster | Complete GPT 16:9 posters illustrate identifier-to-record-to-shelf relationships, including a technical plate where useful. Any data figure has a separate fidelity check. |
| Reading documents | Concept specification organizes entities, lookup/update flow, failure states and open questions; actual research report needs source methods/results/limitations. |
| Website and app | Marketing describes concept scope; app handles unknown identifier, missing shelf, unsaved move and retry without inventing a successful update. |
| Video, voice and interaction | Animate lookup and location change at the narrated anchor. Listen to identifier/symbol pronunciation. Viewer-controlled lookup changes the labeled example shelf. |
| Identity, assets and campaign | Technical editorial style with exact labels, consistent objects and orderly grouping; do not borrow institutional marks or imply lab certification. |
| Data and evidence | No tracking-accuracy/chart from the two fictional identifiers. Real experimental data needs method, units, replicate meaning and uncertainty. |

Review failure example: an arrow connects sample location to a claimed assay result despite no assay in the source. Repair the topology and claims before polishing visual symmetry.
